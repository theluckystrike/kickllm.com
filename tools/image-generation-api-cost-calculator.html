How image API pricing works

OpenAI's GPT Image models bill per output image with three quality tiers. The tier sets how many tokens the image costs to produce — low quality 1024×1024 is about 272 output tokens, medium about 1,056, and high about 4,160 — so moving from low to high quality multiplies the per-image price by roughly 15× on the legacy models, and by 35× on GPT Image 2 square (its $0.006 low tier is unusually cheap).

FLUX models on hosting providers like fal.ai or Replicate bill a flat price per image with no quality tiers, which makes them dramatically cheaper for volume drafts. Aspect ratio changes the token count, and the effect is not a simple multiplier: OpenAI publishes a separate price table per size, and on GPT Image 2 the portrait and landscape outputs are actually cheaper than square at medium and high quality ($0.041/$0.165 vs $0.053/$0.211), while older models charge more for the larger canvas.

Where the money actually goes

  • Retries dominate. A 1.4 attempts-per-final rate adds 40% to every line item; prompt-only iteration on a cheap tier is usually cheaper than re-rolling a high tier.
  • Tier discipline saves the most. Draft on Low or Mini, promote to Medium/High only for finals — teams commonly cut image spend by half or more this way.
  • Aspect ratios compound. Generating everything in portrait at High quality can cost over 27× a square Low image.
