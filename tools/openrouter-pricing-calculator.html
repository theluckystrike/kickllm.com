How OpenRouter pricing works

OpenRouter is a router, not a model provider: it forwards your request to the underlying provider and bills the provider's per-token list price against your credit balance. Your request cost is always (input tokens / 1M) × input rate + (output tokens / 1M) × output rate for the specific variant you call. Prompt caching, batch endpoints, and reasoning tokens each change the effective rate, so check a model's detail page for surcharges before committing a pipeline to it.

Two practical consequences: first, routing the same prompt to a cheaper variant is the single largest lever on your bill — a 10x input-rate gap is common between frontier and efficient tiers. Second, because billing is unified, a single monthly export from OpenRouter gives you per-model spend without stitching together five provider dashboards.

To estimate your own workload: measure average input and output tokens per request from a sample of real traffic (logs, not guesses), multiply by the rates in the table above, then scale by daily request volume. This calculator does that arithmetic in one step, including the monthly projection at 10,000 requests per day.

OpenRouter pricing FAQ

Does OpenRouter add fees on top of model prices? Per-token rates match the provider's list price; OpenRouter earns on credit purchases, where a processing fee applies depending on payment method and amount.

Are free models really free? Variants tagged :free bill $0 but carry lower rate limits and may route to distilled or quantized variants. For production traffic, the paid tier of the same model is the reliable path.

How do I cut my OpenRouter bill fastest? Route non-critical traffic to the cheapest model that clears your quality bar, cache long static prefixes, and cap output tokens — output rates are typically 3-5x input rates.
